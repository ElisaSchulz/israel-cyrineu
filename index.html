Israel Molina Cyrineu

PhD Candidate, Department of Plant and Soil Sciences
Oklahoma State University · expected Fall 2026

I study why winter wheat loses yield when weather forces farmers to plant late — and which varieties and management decisions hold that yield when it does.

My research sits at the intersection of crop physiology and agronomy. In the US Southern Great Plains, floods and droughts increasingly push wheat sowing weeks past the optimal window, and the standard advice has been to compensate by seeding more heavily. Working across multiple locations and growing seasons in Oklahoma, I test that assumption directly: which physiological traits actually explain the delayed-sowing yield penalty, whether short-season genotypes recover it, and whether higher seeding rates help at all. The answers turn out to be more useful — and more surprising — than the recommendations they replace.

I work with the Small Grains Extension and Research program at Oklahoma State University, advised by Dr. Amanda de Oliveira Silva. Alongside my dissertation, I contribute to the variety testing and extension work that puts these results in front of Oklahoma wheat producers each season.

I grew up in São Paulo, Brazil, and earned my B.S. in agronomy at the University of São Paulo in 2021, where I worked on precision agriculture and spraying technologies with the Precision Agriculture Group (gMAP). I joined the Small Grains team at Oklahoma State in January 2022, completed my M.S. there in 2023, and began the PhD in 2024.
